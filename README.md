# Project Overview

This project presents an experimental study examining whether note-taking practices and learning styles influence students' comprehension and memory recall.

The study investigated three learning modalities—auditory, reading, and visual—and compared students who were allowed to take notes with those who were not. The objective was to determine whether either factor, or their interaction, produced meaningful differences in students' learning outcomes.

The study was conducted using a 2×3 factorial experimental design with 36 participants, randomly assigned across learning-style and note-taking conditions. Comprehension and memory recall were measured through a 10-item post-test consisting of five comprehension and five memory-recall questions.

## Research Objectives

- Determine the effect of note-taking on students' comprehension and memory recall.
- Identify differences in performance among auditory, reading, and visual learning groups.
- Examine the interaction between learning style and note-taking on comprehension and memory recall.

## Methodology

Research Design: 2×3 Factorial Design

Independent Variables:

- Learning Style
  - Auditory
  - Reading
  - Visual
- Note-Taking
  - With Notes
  - Without Notes

Dependent Variables:

- Comprehension
- Memory Recall

Sample Size: 36 participants

Participants were randomly assigned to the experimental conditions. Each participant completed a pre-test, received a passage through their assigned learning modality, and completed a post-test measuring comprehension and memory recall.

## Statistical Analysis

The study applied Two-Way MANOVA to examine the effects of learning style and note-taking on the combined dependent variables.

Before conducting the MANOVA, several statistical assumptions were assessed, including:

- Univariate outliers
- Multivariate outliers
- Normality
- Linearity
- Homogeneity of variance
- Homogeneity of covariance
- Multicollinearity

The assumption tests generally supported proceeding with the MANOVA despite some deviations from normality.

## Key Findings

The Two-Way MANOVA found no statistically significant effect of learning style or note-taking on the combined measures of comprehension and memory recall.

- Learning Style: Wilks' Λ = 0.886, p = 0.469
- Note-Taking: Wilks' Λ = 0.938, p = 0.392

The between-subjects analyses likewise showed no statistically significant effects on either comprehension or memory recall.

The study therefore failed to reject the null hypothesis, indicating that, within this sample, students performed similarly regardless of their assigned learning style or note-taking condition.

Descriptive Results

Measure| Mean| Standard Deviation
Comprehension| 3.92| 0.806
Memory Recall| 3.97| 1.276

Memory recall showed greater variability among participants than comprehension scores.

## Limitations

The study was subject to several limitations, including:

- Relatively small sample size (n = 36)
- Note-taking quality and methods were not controlled
- Learning-style categories may oversimplify individual differences
- Potential differences in prior knowledge and motivation
- Environmental distractions were not fully controlled

These limitations may have contributed to the absence of statistically significant findings.

## Recommendations

Future research should consider:

1. Increasing the sample size to improve statistical power.
2. Standardizing note-taking techniques across participants.
3. Refining the classification of learning styles.
4. Controlling for prior knowledge, motivation, and environmental factors.
5. Designing assessment tasks that more directly correspond to the learning modalities being investigated.

## Overview

This study examined the relationship between household socioeconomic characteristics and food insecurity among households in Mandaluyong City. Specifically, it investigated whether monthly household income was significantly associated with household food insecurity, as measured using the Household Food Insecurity Access Scale (HFIAS).

Using data from 50 household respondents, the study analyzed four key socioeconomic variables: estimated monthly household income, household size, number of income earners, and monthly household food expenditure. A cross-sectional research design was used, with respondents selected through purposive and convenience sampling. Data were collected through a structured questionnaire administered face-to-face.

Because the data showed substantial deviations from the assumptions required for traditional ANCOVA, the study applied Quade’s Rank-Based Nonparametric ANCOVA to compare food insecurity across income groups while accounting for relevant covariates. Additional statistical analyses included frequency and percentage distributions, Pearson correlation, multiple regression, post-hoc comparisons, and residual analysis.

## Key Findings

The analysis showed that monthly household income had a statistically significant effect on food insecurity, with the lowest-income group experiencing significantly higher adjusted HFIAS scores than the middle- and highest-income groups, even after controlling for the covariate. The model explained approximately 19.9% of the variance in the outcome.

Among the socioeconomic covariates, household size was the only significant predictor of food insecurity. Larger households were associated with higher HFIAS scores, while the number of income earners and monthly food expenditure were not statistically significant predictors. The regression model explained approximately 17.7% of the variance in the ranked outcome.

Overall, the findings suggest that income level and household size are important factors associated with household food insecurity within the sample. The study recommends targeted food assistance, income-support programs, and resource allocation that considers both household income and family size.

## Thesis Overview

Digital Food Platforms and Household Food Security: Understanding Access, Effectiveness, and Barriers in Mandaluyong City is a quantitative research study that examines how online food delivery platforms contribute to household food access and food security in an urban community.

The study investigates how factors such as convenience and accessibility, affordability, digital literacy and payment preferences, and food quality and availability influence the use of digital food platforms. It also examines barriers related to financial limitations, digital and technical difficulties, service reliability, and trust and security.

Using survey data from households in Mandaluyong City, the research applies descriptive and correlational statistical methods to analyze respondents' demographic characteristics, patterns of online food delivery usage, and food security conditions.

The study demonstrates the application of statistical analysis, survey research, data interpretation, and quantitative research methods to understand the relationship between digital technology and household food security.

## Key Areas

- Quantitative & descriptive-correlational research
- Survey data collection and analysis
- Household food security assessment
- Digital platform usage analysis
- Demographic and socioeconomic analysis
- Statistical testing and correlation analysis
- Data interpretation and visualization
