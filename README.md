# Project Overview

This project presents an experimental study examining whether note-taking practices and learning styles influence students' comprehension and memory recall.

The study investigated three learning modalities—auditory, reading, and visual—and compared students who were allowed to take notes with those who were not. The objective was to determine whether either factor, or their interaction, produced meaningful differences in students' learning outcomes.

The study was conducted using a 2×3 factorial experimental design with 36 participants, randomly assigned across learning-style and note-taking conditions. Comprehension and memory recall were measured through a 10-item post-test consisting of five comprehension and five memory-recall questions.

## Research Objectives

- Determine the effect of note-taking on students' comprehension and memory recall.
- Identify differences in performance among auditory, reading, and visual learning groups.
- Examine the interaction between learning style and note-taking on comprehension and memory recall.

## Methodology

Research Design: 2×3 Factorial Design

Independent Variables:

- Learning Style
  - Auditory
  - Reading
  - Visual
- Note-Taking
  - With Notes
  - Without Notes

Dependent Variables:

- Comprehension
- Memory Recall

Sample Size: 36 participants

Participants were randomly assigned to the experimental conditions. Each participant completed a pre-test, received a passage through their assigned learning modality, and completed a post-test measuring comprehension and memory recall.

## Statistical Analysis

The study applied Two-Way MANOVA to examine the effects of learning style and note-taking on the combined dependent variables.

Before conducting the MANOVA, several statistical assumptions were assessed, including:

- Univariate outliers
- Multivariate outliers
- Normality
- Linearity
- Homogeneity of variance
- Homogeneity of covariance
- Multicollinearity

The assumption tests generally supported proceeding with the MANOVA despite some deviations from normality.

## Key Findings

The Two-Way MANOVA found no statistically significant effect of learning style or note-taking on the combined measures of comprehension and memory recall.

- Learning Style: Wilks' Λ = 0.886, p = 0.469
- Note-Taking: Wilks' Λ = 0.938, p = 0.392

The between-subjects analyses likewise showed no statistically significant effects on either comprehension or memory recall.

The study therefore failed to reject the null hypothesis, indicating that, within this sample, students performed similarly regardless of their assigned learning style or note-taking condition.

Descriptive Results

Measure| Mean| Standard Deviation
Comprehension| 3.92| 0.806
Memory Recall| 3.97| 1.276

Memory recall showed greater variability among participants than comprehension scores.

## Limitations

The study was subject to several limitations, including:

- Relatively small sample size (n = 36)
- Note-taking quality and methods were not controlled
- Learning-style categories may oversimplify individual differences
- Potential differences in prior knowledge and motivation
- Environmental distractions were not fully controlled

These limitations may have contributed to the absence of statistically significant findings.

## Recommendations

Future research should consider:

1. Increasing the sample size to improve statistical power.
2. Standardizing note-taking techniques across participants.
3. Refining the classification of learning styles.
4. Controlling for prior knowledge, motivation, and environmental factors.
5. Designing assessment tasks that more directly correspond to the learning modalities being investigated.
